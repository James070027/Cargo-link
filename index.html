      Before you book

      Have these ready

      • What you are moving and the weight or number of items (sacks, boxes, furniture, litres).
      • Exact pickup place and delivery place, and the date and time you want.
      • Whether you need loading helpers, and if the road to the pickup or delivery place is narrow or muddy.
      • Your phone number, so drivers can call if they get lost.

      Which vehicle?

      • Parcels and documents: boda boda cargo.
      • Small shop stock, a few boxes or furniture: cargo tricycle, van or pickup.
      • House or office move: canter or box body.
      • Building materials, sand, stones or soil: tipper or flatbed.
      • Big loads and long distance: Fuso, Isuzu or Hino, Sinotruk (HOWO) or trailer.
      • Containers, fuel, cold goods, animals, cars, concrete or machinery: container truck, fuel tanker, refrigerated truck, livestock truck, car carrier, concrete mixer, crane lorry or low-bed trailer.

        How to choose a good driver

        • Verified means the admin has seen the driver licence and number plate. Choose Verified drivers first when you can.
        • Look at the photos of the truck and compare the number plate with the truck that arrives.
        • Check that the driver serves your route. The card shows the base town, routes and towns he works in.
        • Free today means the driver says his truck is free now. Returning empty means he is travelling back empty and can give you a better price on that road.
        • Top spot drivers pay to be shown first. It does not by itself mean the best driver, so always compare.
        • Message two or three drivers, describe the load, and compare their price and time.

        Before the truck is loaded

        • Agree the price, the loading time, who loads and offloads, and what is included (fuel, tolls, waiting).
        • Take a photo of the goods and of the truck plate. Keep the WhatsApp chat as your record.
        • Pay a small deposit if needed, and the balance on delivery after checking the goods.

        Frequently asked questions

        Support: call 0700 275758 or chat on WhatsApp 0766 791 805.

        What is this app?

        It is a directory and job board that helps customers find truck, canter and pickup drivers. We do not own vehicles and we do not carry goods ourselves.

        Who sets the price?

        Each driver quotes his own price. Agree the price, the loading time and what is included before the truck is loaded.

        How do I know a driver is genuine?

        A Verified driver has shown us his driving licence and vehicle number plate. Always check the number plate on the vehicle matches what you were told. Drivers not marked Verified are listed but not checked.

        Do customers pay Cargo Link?

        No. Drivers pay a monthly listing fee. You pay the driver directly, for the trip.

        Something went wrong. What do I do?

        Use the Report a problem button at the bottom of the app. We can remove drivers from the list. We cannot promise to recover losses or settle payments between customers and drivers.

        Terms of use

        1. Cargo Link is a directory. Every transport agreement is only between the customer and the driver or truck owner.

        2. We check what we say we check (licence and number plate for Verified drivers), but we do not guarantee any driver, vehicle, price, delivery or the condition of goods.

        3. Good practice: do not pay the full amount in advance, note the number plate, take photos of the goods when loaded and when delivered, and get the receiver's name.

        4. Drivers must give true information and keep their listing up to date. We may hide or remove any listing, for example after complaints.

        5. Listing fees paid by drivers cover the listing only. They are not payment for any trip.

        6. Do not use the app for illegal goods or to mislead other people.

        Privacy

        What we handle: the names, phone numbers, places and goods details you type into the app. A trip request goes to the driver through WhatsApp. When a job is posted on the job board, its details are stored online, and the customer's phone number is not shown publicly.

        Driver details (name, phone number, base, routes, vehicle photos) are shown on the app with the driver's agreement so that customers can contact him.

        We do not sell your details. To ask us to correct or remove your details, use the Need help button at the bottom of the app.

        How live tracking works

        For customers

        • Ask the driver for his tracking code or the tracking link. He gets it from the Live tracking box on his driver page.
        • Type the code above and tap Show the truck. A map opens with the truck marked on it.
        • The map refreshes every 10 seconds. Under the map you see how long ago the position was updated.
        • Tap Open in Google Maps to see roads around the truck and to work out how far it still has to go.

        If you cannot see the truck

        • Check the code. It has letters and numbers, and capital or small letters do not matter.
        • The driver may not have pressed Start live tracking yet, or he closed the page.
        • The driver phone may have no internet, or GPS or location is switched off. In tunnels and some rural areas the position can be old for a few minutes.
        • If the last update is more than 5 minutes old, call the driver, or call our support on 0700 275758.

        For drivers

        • Open your driver page, find Live tracking and tap Start live tracking. Allow location when the phone asks.
        • Keep the page open and the phone charged. Turn off battery saver for your browser or the app, or the phone may pause tracking.
        • Send the tracking link to the customer with one tap, then tap Stop when the goods are delivered.

        Privacy and safety

        • Your position is shared only while tracking is ON. Only the latest position is kept, not the full history of your trip.
        • Share the code only with the person receiving the goods.
        • Tracking helps you follow the trip. It is not a guarantee. Confirm the number plate, and do not pay the balance until the goods arrive and you have checked them.

        Before a long trip

        • Check tyres, brakes, oil and water before you load.
        • Agree the price, the loading place and the offloading place in writing on WhatsApp.
        • Carry your driving permit, vehicle papers, insurance and the goods papers.
        • Plan fuel stops and a rest every 4 hours. A tired driver is a danger on the road.
        • Share your trip with someone: tell them the route and when you expect to arrive.
        • For border trips, ask the customer for the customs papers before you leave.

        Travel tips for trucks

        • Plan around Kampala traffic. The roads to Entebbe, Jinja and the Northern Bypass are very slow at peak hours, so leave early or late.
        • Expect weighbridge stops on long routes. Carry the vehicle papers, the driver licence and the delivery papers.
        • On long trips plan fuel, rest and meals. Tired drivers cause accidents, so avoid driving through the night when you can.
        • In the rainy season some roads become slippery or blocked. Ask the driver about the road before you load.

        Cross-border trips

        • Common border posts are Malaba and Busia (Kenya), Katuna (Rwanda), Mutukula (Tanzania), Elegu (South Sudan) and Bunagana (Congo).
        • Ask the driver if he has cross-border permits and insurance, and who pays border fees and clearing agents.
        • Allow extra days for queues and inspection, and keep copies of invoices and packing lists.

        What changes the price

        • Fuel: when fuel goes up, drivers quote higher. These ranges already assume high fuel prices.
        • Distance and road: bad roads and hills cost more fuel and time.
        • Weight and loading: heavy loads, and loading or offloading help, add to the price.
        • Return load: a driver who gets a load back may charge less.
        • Border trips: border fees, papers and waiting time are usually extra.
        • Waiting and parking: long waiting at the site is normally charged.

        Always agree the price, the loading place and the offloading place with the driver before the truck moves.

        What changes the price

        • Distance and road: longer trips cost more. Bad roads and hills use more fuel and time.
        • Weight and size: a full load needs a bigger truck than a half load.
        • Fuel price: when fuel goes up, transport prices usually go up too.
        • Loading and offloading: ask if helpers are included. Waiting time at the site can be charged.
        • Coming back empty: the driver may charge for the empty return, unless he has a load coming back. Ask about Returning empty drivers.
        • Tolls, weighbridges and border fees: ask if they are inside the price.
        • Urgent or night trips often cost more than planned day trips.

        How to get a fair price

        • Ask two or three drivers with the same details so the prices can be compared.
        • Ask for the price in writing on WhatsApp, with what is included.
        • A very low price can mean an old truck, extra charges later, or no real insurance. Ask questions before you agree.
        • The price guide above is only a rough guide in thousands of shillings. The driver agrees the final price with you.
